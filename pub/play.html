Touring Artists · Musos · Travelling Bands

Play The Flat.

Chasing real rooms and real crowds? Bring a gig to a 1938 Art Deco pub in a historic mining village — where a live act is the event of the week and the whole town shows up.

Why Here

A room worth the drive

Captains Flat is an old mining town in the hills between Canberra and the coast — and the pub is the heart of it. If you’re touring the region, this is the kind of gig people talk about for months after.

01

A Real Room

Art Deco walls, a wood fire, and a bar that was Australia’s longest when it was built in 1938. Play to a crowd that came to listen — not a wall of poker machines.

02

The Town Shows Up

The Flat is the village’s dining room, meeting place and watering hole all in one. A gig here isn’t background noise — it’s the night out.

03

On the Run

A worthwhile stop between Canberra and the Braidwood–coast leg. Break up the drive, fill the room, and wake up somewhere with a story.

The Gig

What a night here looks like

We keep it simple and we keep it friendly. Solo and duo acoustic sets, Friday and Saturday nights on the bar side, lazy Sunday sessions in the afternoon — whatever suits your rig and the road you’re on.

Bring your own setup and tell us what you need — space, power, timing, a door deal. We’d rather have a chat and make it work than bury you in a booking form.

And if the crowd loves you (they will), the 1938 whisky bar is a very good place for a nightcap.

Stay the Night

Make a weekend of it

Travelling in the tour van? There’s donation-based camping with hot showers at Wilkins Memorial Park, a short stumble from the bar. Or put the band in proper beds — heritage rooms upstairs from $90 a night.

Either way, we’ll feed you: hot pub meals Wednesday to Sunday, and breakfast available for rooms. Ask nicely and we might even float you a glass on the famous wall.
